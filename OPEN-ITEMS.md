## For the test evaluator

### 1. L-01 · How many invoices arrive scanned?

**What the samples showed.**

- **Native:** `INV-001` to `INV-010` have a full text layer.
- **Scanned:** `INV-011` is a scanned image with no text at all.
- **Mixed:** `INV-012` combines an image with a text layer.

So both kinds of PDF exist.

**Provisional decision.**

- Native PDFs that follow the sample layout are read with `pdfplumber`.
- A PDF with less text per page than the configured minimum is rejected with the reason `pdf_without_text_layer`. The rejection appears in the job status and in the export, and the invoice is never coded.
- OCR moves to a separate spec.

**Alternatives considered.**

- *OCR with Tesseract:* deferred. Extraction would become the largest source of error in the system, and there is only one scanned sample to measure it against.
- *A vision model reading the scan:* rejected. The model would be reading the billed entity, which is the most expensive step to get wrong, and its output cannot be checked against a closed list.
- *Accepting the PDF and coding whatever little text comes out:* rejected, because it produces wrong codes with no warning.

**Question.** Roughly what share of real invoices arrive scanned? Is OCR needed before production?

### 2. L-02 · Are the samples synthetic, and what are their expected codes?

**What the samples showed.** Twelve samples arrived. Between them, `INV-001` to `INV-010` cover almost every edge case in the brief:

- exact entity;
- `LDA` in capitals without a hyphen;
- third-party clients;
- a client billed "c/o" AMT;
- a sister company with a different tax number;
- one line with no match in OUTLAYSP.

**Provisional decision.**

- **The samples stay outside the repository.** The golden set reads them from a local path set by an environment variable. Tests use synthetic replicas generated by a script and labelled as synthetic.
- **The expected results live in an editable configuration file.** They use the codes of the current catalog and can change with it. The initial proposal is below.
- **`make golden` measures the deterministic stages only:** extraction, entity, target sheet, line types and rejections. Measuring item codes with the real model is a separate manual command, outside the test suite.
- **No accuracy figure is published** until the expected results are confirmed.

### 3. L-08 · What happens to a line with no match in OUTLAYS? And what role does O00052 play?

**Why it matters.** The brief restricts the code `O00999` to the OUTLAYSP sheet and says nothing about OUTLAYS. The catalog has a generic item in OUTLAYS, `O00052 Outras Despesas / Other Expenses`, which can work as a fallback in practice.

**What the samples showed.** None of the ten coded samples has an OUTLAYS line without a matching item. The case is still open.

**Provisional decision.**

- The line gets **no code**, `LOW` confidence, and is flagged with the reason `no_match_in_outlays`.
- O00052 is a valid item and the model may choose it. When it does, the line is always `LOW` and flagged with `generic_catalog_item`.

**Alternatives considered.**

- *Assigning `O00999`:* rejected, because it breaks the rule in the brief.
- *Assigning the best available candidate:* rejected, because it produces a wrong code that looks right.
- *Using O00052 as an automatic fallback for OUTLAYS:* rejected without confirmation, because it would mean filling a gap on our own initiative.

**Question.** Should an OUTLAYS line with no match get no code? Is O00052 the intended fallback for OUTLAYS?

### 4. Invoices with an ambiguous entity get an empty target sheet

**Why it matters.** The `Target_Sheet` column only allows `OUTLAYS` or `OUTLAYSP`. An invoice whose entity cannot be determined safely has no target sheet, so we leave the column empty. This departs from the output format, so it has to be declared.

An entity is ambiguous when:

- the billed-entity block is missing;
- there is more than one billed-entity block;
- the name is similar to AMT's but not the same;
- the name equals AMT's but the tax number does not (see item 7).

**Provisional decision.**

- The invoice is **not coded**.
- Its lines are output with:
  - `Exact_Entity_Match = NO`;
  - an empty `Target_Sheet`;
  - no code;
  - `LOW` confidence;
  - a flag for review.
- A reviewer resolves the entity, with a mandatory note, and the invoice is then coded.

**Alternatives considered.**

- *Routing to OUTLAYSP because it is "not a match":* rejected. It would apply `O00999` by default to invoices that may be AMT's.
- *Leaving the invoice out of the export:* rejected, because it hides information from the reviewer.
- *Asking the language model to break the tie:* rejected. The rule for refusing third parties is strict, and accepting a sister company by mistake has tax consequences.

**Question.** Does the evaluator accept this departure from the format?

## For AMT

### 5. AGT customs assessment notices

**What the samples showed.** `INV-012` is a customs assessment notice from AGT, not a supplier invoice. It differs from the invoices in four ways:

- **No billed entity.** There is no "Bill To" block, only a "Declarant" (a private individual) and a "Consignee" (the client).
- **Lines.** The lines are customs duties and fees: import duty, customs service fee, statistical fee, and VAT on import.
- **Formats.** Amounts use the `100.000` style and dates the `dd/mm/yyyy` style.
- **Mixed content.** The file holds an image and a text layer.

**Provisional decision.** The document type is detected, and these notices are rejected with the reason `unsupported_document_type`. The rejection is visible in the job status and in the export.

**Alternatives considered.**

- *Reading the notice with the invoice layout:* rejected. It would produce garbage, or at best an ambiguous entity.
- *Classifying the duties as tax lines and leaving them uncoded:* rejected. Customs duties are exactly what AMT pays on behalf of a client, and should be coded (probably `O00002 Alfândegas`, in OUTLAYSP).

**Question.** Do these notices come through the same channel as invoices? If they do, they get their own spec, with "Consignee" as the billed entity and the duties coded in OUTLAYSP.

### 6. Reading of "c/o" in the billed-entity block

**What the samples showed.** In `INV-002`, the billed-entity block reads "Operadora Sintética de Petróleo Angola" on the first line and "c/o Advanced Maritime Transports" on the second.

**Provisional decision.**

- The billed entity is **the first line of the block**.
- A line starting with "c/o" or "a/c" is recorded as "care of" and shown in the notes, but never enters the entity check.
- `INV-002` is therefore not a match and is coded against OUTLAYSP.

**Alternatives considered.**

- *Treating the "c/o" line as a second billed entity:* rejected. The invoice would become ambiguous with `multiple_billed_entities`, although it plainly belongs to the client.
- *Letting the "c/o" line count as an AMT match:* rejected. It would route a client's expense to OUTLAYS.

**Question.** Is this reading right? Is an invoice in a client's name delivered "c/o" AMT always an expense on the client's behalf?

### 7. L-04 · Sister companies, shipping lines, and AMT's tax number

**Why it matters.** This item has the largest effect on correctness. A freight forwarder's sister company almost always has a name very close to AMT's.

**What the samples showed.** `INV-010` is billed to "AMT - Empresa Irmã Sintética Limitada":

- It has the same address as AMT.
- Its tax number is almost identical to AMT's: only the last digits differ.

Here the name differs enough to be caught. A sister company named "Advanced Maritime Transports - Agentes de Navegação" would only be caught by its tax number.

**Provisional decision.**

- The denylist lives in `config/entity_denylist.yaml` and is checked **before** any approximate comparison. Its contents are not invented, so it stays empty until there is an answer.
- AMT's tax number is set in configuration (`SEXTANTE_TARGET_ENTITY_NIF`), with the value shown on the samples. The repository holds only a synthetic value.
- The tax number is used as follows:

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Billed entity's tax number is present and **differs** from AMT's | Never a match. If the name equals AMT's, the invoice is ambiguous with `entity_nif_mismatch` |
| Tax number equals AMT's | Never enough on its own; the name must still match |
| No tax number | The name rules apply |

- Any other name similar to AMT's but not the same is ambiguous and goes to review.

**Cost while there is no answer.** More invoices in the review queue. None are routed wrongly.

**Requests.**

- The exact names of the sister companies, shipping lines and recurring clients, as they appear on invoices.
- Confirmation that the tax number shown on the samples is AMT's.

Some pairs, however, do not say the same thing:

### 9. L-06 · VAT, discount, subtotal and total lines

**Why it matters.** A "IVA 14%" line treated as a service gets an arbitrary code with artificially high confidence.

**What the samples showed.**

- VAT never appears as a line in the table. It is a per-line percentage column (0%, 7% or 14%) and a summary row below the table.
- Subtotal, VAT and total always sit outside the table.
- The only "tax-like" lines are the customs duties on the AGT notice (item 5), and those **should** be coded.

**Provisional decision.**

| Line type | Handling |
|---|---|
| Service | Coded |
| VAT and discount | Output with no code, `HIGH` confidence and a note giving the type, already accepted and not sent to review |
| Subtotal and total | Left out of the output, because they are not invoice lines. Kept only in the audit record |

- The VAT pattern recognises **only the supplier's VAT**: "IVA", "VAT", "Imposto sobre o Valor Acrescentado". It never matches "taxa" or "imposto" on their own. A line such as "Taxa de pilotagem" (`INV-003`) is a service.
- Each line's VAT percentage is stored as an attribute of the line.

**Alternative considered.** The earlier policy sent every non-service line to review. It was rejected because almost every invoice has VAT, so the review queue would fill up with easy cases.

**Question.** Can a VAT, discount, rounding or exchange-rate line ever appear inside the line table of a real invoice?

### 10. L-09 · Can an invoice have more than one billed entity?

**What the samples showed.** No sample has two billed-entity blocks. The "c/o" case in `INV-002` is a single entity with a care-of line, not two entities (see item 6).

**Provisional decision.** We assume one billed-entity block per invoice. If extraction finds more than one, the invoice is ambiguous, with the reason `multiple_billed_entities`, and is not coded.

**If mixed invoices exist.** Routing would be done per line instead of per invoice. That changes the data model and the output.

### 11. L-10 · Language, currency and VAT rate

**What the samples showed.**

- **Languages.** The invoices use bilingual labels, and their lines are in Portuguese or English.
- **Currencies.** There are two, AOA and USD, written as `1,206.00`. The AGT notice uses `100.000`.
- **VAT rate.** In `INV-007` and `INV-009`, the line's VAT rate matches the VAT group of the expected catalog item: 14% for Sonils and 7% for Hotel.

**Provisional decision.**

- The system supports Portuguese and English.
- The currency is extracted and recorded but does **not** influence the choice of code.
- The line's VAT rate is recorded but does **not** influence the choice of code or the confidence.

**Questions.**

- Are there invoices in other languages or currencies?
- Does the currency ever change which code to assign?
- Can the match between a line's VAT rate and an item's VAT group be relied on, so that it could be used as a confidence signal?

### 12. Credit notes and reissued invoices

**Why it matters.** Duplicate detection by file content only catches the same file uploaded twice. The same invoice sent again in another format would go through with no warning, which is the first step towards paying twice.

**Provisional decision.** An invoice with the same supplier and number as one already uploaded, but different content, is still coded and is flagged as a **possible duplicate**, with a reference to the earlier one.

**Alternative considered.** Blocking the upload: rejected, because a credit note may reuse the original invoice's number.

**Questions.**

- Do credit notes come through this same channel?
- How are they told apart from an invoice?

### 13. Supplier identification outside the sample layout

**What the samples showed.** All ten invoices in the sample layout carry two tax numbers: the supplier's and the billed entity's. The supplier's **name**, however, sits in the top banner, not in the "FROM / FORNECEDOR" block, which holds only the address and the tax number.

**Provisional decision.** The invoice identifier is `<supplier>/<invoice number>`. The supplier is identified by its tax number when one is extracted, and by its normalised name otherwise.

**Question.** Do invoices from other suppliers, outside this layout, also carry the tax number?

### 14. L-07 · Authentication

**Provisional decision.**

### 15. L-12 · Model budget

**Provisional decision.**

- One model call per invoice.
- The cost per invoice is measured and published in `README.md`.
- Token usage is recorded per job.

**Note.** The model is sent the whole target sheet once per invoice: 44 items for OUTLAYS, 20 for OUTLAYSP. The item list is the same in every call, which makes it a candidate for prompt caching. The samples have one or two lines per invoice, fewer than the six to ten assumed in the sizing.

**Question.** Is there a monthly cost limit?

### 16. L-11 · Data retention

**Provisional decision for local use.**

- The original file is kept for as long as its job exists.
- The extracted text is kept, so invoices can be reprocessed without a new upload.
- The audit record is never deleted.

**Proposal for production.**

- The audit record is kept for 24 months.
- The original file is deleted once its coding has been accepted.

**Note.** `INV-012` names a private individual, the customs declarant. Retention therefore also covers personal data.

**Question.** Which retention periods apply, from a legal standpoint?
